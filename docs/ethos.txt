## 0) Principles & Non‑Negotiables

1. **Custodial by design**
   TIRO seeks to be a trusted custodian for human memory. Neutrality means non-appropriation and fair handling of contested accounts, not absence of curation. Longevity, provenance, community control, and scoped consent take priority over virality.

2. **Provider‑agnostic, universally modular**
   Everything integrates via adapters: storage, search, auth, payments, analytics, LLMs, and messaging.

3. **Privacy, dignity, consent**
   Consent is explicit, traceable, and revocable. PII minimization, data sovereignty, and export are first‑class.

4. **Accessible & inclusive**
   WCAG 2.2 AA (min), screen‑reader parity, captions/transcripts, color‑contrast budgets, and motion‑safe defaults.

5. **Performance budgets**
   Core Web Vitals targets: LCP ≤ 2.5s, INP ≤ 200ms, CLS ≤ 0.1 on 4G mid‑tier phones worldwide.

6. **Observability & auditability**
   Versioned records, tamper-evident audit events with minimal nonsensitive identifiers, and transparent model cards for AI features. Sensitive testimony, consent documents, personal contacts, and withdrawn content belong in restricted stores with retention controls, never permanent public logs.

7. **Community capacity and language inclusion**
   Localize critical consent and withdrawal flows before collection. Support and credit community translators without turning cultural participation into a speculative economy.

## 3) Core Feature Modules (Universal & Swappable)

### 3.1 Identity & Roles

* Passwordless email or passkeys; optional SSO (Google/Microsoft/Apple).
* Roles: **Visitor**, **Contributor**, **Curator**, **Archivist**, **Translator**, **Partner**, **Staff**.
* Fine‑grained policies via RBAC/ABAC (e.g., regional collections, sensitive content gates).

### 3.2 Memory Ingestion

* Guided multi‑step uploader (media → metadata → consent → review).
* Accepts images, audio, video, text, PDFs. Auto‑transcode, checksums, virus scanning.
* **Field sets**: title, summary, subjects, collectors, contributors, location (geo), languages, capture date, cultural context, content warnings, source authority, legal rights where known, signer capacity, permitted uses, access restrictions, and withdrawal instructions.
* Submission is quarantined by default. Collector, contributor, subject, source authority, legal rights holder, and authorized representative are separate capacities; one person may hold several only with supporting evidence. A collector's upload or signature is not proof of authority over everyone recorded.
* A subject is a person represented in the material; identify applicable privacy, dignity, and consent interests separately from authorship or cultural authority. Minimize public identifying information.
* Offline‑friendly chunked uploads; resumable; background processing.

### 3.3 Consent & Rights Registry

The policy at `/community-rights` supplies the governing principles. The following operational definitions and evidence requirements implement them; they do not expand a grant of consent.

**Capacity and authority**

* **Source authority**: person, community, or body with cultural or intellectual authority underlying the material; record shared, disputed, uncertain, or unassigned authority explicitly.
* **Legal rights holder**: holder of an identified applicable legal right (for example copyright), with evidence and jurisdiction where known. Cultural authority neither depends on, nor substitutes for, establishing that legal right. Unknown legal rights require assessment, not an assumption of clearance.
* **Authorized representative / signer capacity**: whose interests the signer may represent, for which material and uses, on what basis, and for how long. Keep the mandate or verification receipt restricted. A signer cannot grant another party's rights by assertion alone.
* Identify all applicable authorizations for each proposed use, including subject interests and collective protocols. Unresolved authority prevents the affected use; quarantine and preservation-only retention also require their own lawful, disclosed basis.

**Use permissions**

| Use | Meaning and authorization boundary |
| --- | --- |
| Recording / collection | Capture or receive specified material; does not imply retention or public access |
| Preservation | Retain, copy for integrity, restore, and migrate within specified custody terms; no automatic publication |
| Publication | Make specified material and metadata accessible to an authorized audience and through named channels |
| Research | Permit defined analysis or researcher access under separate access and output conditions |
| Derivatives | Translate, edit, adapt, or create other versions only within the approved scope; track the source and permission |
| Model training | Include material in training, fine-tuning, or evaluation datasets only when specifically authorized for that purpose |
| Synthetic reproduction | Generate imitations, reconstructed performances, or voice/likeness reproductions only under distinct explicit authorization |
| Commercial reuse | License or otherwise exploit commercially only with explicit authority and approved terms; educational access does not grant it |

Biometric analysis and materially different computational uses require distinct explicit scope as well. Transcription and translation services must satisfy the permitted processing terms; vendors receive no independent training or reuse right. Permission to train does not grant synthetic reproduction or commercial reuse.

**Per-record receipt**

Record signer identity and capacity; applicable authority and rights; evidence references; record/version covered; date, jurisdiction, language and comprehension method; purposes and audiences; media and metadata permitted; restrictions; duration and review/renewal dates; withdrawal requester and decision authority; retention grounds and periods; backup/copy handling; and contact procedure. Publish only the safe consent summary, never the underlying identity documents.

Grants are versioned and use-specific. “Not granted,” “unknown,” “disputed,” and “withdrawn” are not equivalent to permission. Renewed or expanded use requires fresh applicable authorization and review. §12 defines the action and response workflow.

### 3.4 Processing & Enrichment

* Transcription (speech‑to‑text), translation memory, entity metadata (people/places/keywords).
* Human‑in‑the‑loop verification queues; provenance chain maintained. Permission checks occur before processing and before sending content to a vendor. Human review distinguishes source testimony, transcription, translation, and TIRO interpretation; automation cannot confer authority.

### 3.5 Curation & Moderation

* Queues for review, sensitivity flags, cultural protocols, redaction tools (blur, bleep, crop, transcript redaction).
* Dual‑control approvals for sensitive materials before the first real collection release; documented context and reasons for selection. Ethics or curator approval cannot expand source permissions. Disagreements remain attributed and are not resolved by silently overwriting a source account.

### 3.6 Discovery & Navigation

* Universal **Search** (keyword, facets: language, region, era, medium, tags, consent).
* **Pathways**: narrative playlists/maps; embeddable; exportable.
* **Timelines** & **Geo‑maps** with clustering and offline tiles.

### 3.7 Participation & Programs

* Assignments for students/volunteers; checklists; mentor review; evidence-based verification receipts identifying what was checked, by whom, and with what limitations. Verification is not a popularity or contributor-reputation score.
* Translator workflows with memory‑based suggestions; glossary.

### 3.8 Notifications & Messaging

* In‑app and email digests. Event hooks (webhooks) for integrations.

### 3.9 Analytics & Transparency

* Collection metrics, community benefit, preservation integrity, translation coverage, consent/withdrawal handling, accessibility, uptime, and audits. Demo records are excluded from real collection counts, pilot results, impact reports, and donor evidence.

### 3.10 Sustainability & Export

* Preservation export (JSONL + authorized media + checksums + provenance + consent/restriction/withdrawal state) and tested restoration are prerequisites for real collection (§6.1). Use open formats; export capability is not redistribution permission.
* Staff preservation packages include only material the destination is authorized to hold and the necessary restricted policy metadata. Public exports must omit restricted content, identifiers, and sensitive metadata. A media package without its permissions and lifecycle history is incomplete.
* External deposits require approved destination custody terms, access and retention limits, withdrawal notification, derivative controls, and acceptance of source obligations. Cold storage cannot erase those obligations. Partner-copy duties are tracked under §12.

## 4) Data & Domain Model (proposed operational schema)

**Entities** (IDs are UUIDv7; all write operations are versioned):

* `Memory` — core object; `recordKind`: demo|pilot|collection; review and verification evidence; mediaRefs[], transcript, languages[], tags[], geo, era, subjects[], collectors[], contributors[], authorityRefs[], legalRightsRefs[], consentRefs[], accessPolicyRef, withdrawalTermsRef, provenanceRefs[], related[]. `publicationStatus` and `custodyStatus` are separate (§12).
* `AuthorityClaim` — source authority, shared/disputed/unknown status, claimant, scope, evidenceRef, reviewer, and resolution/appeal history.
* `LegalRight` — right type, holder, scope, jurisdiction, evidence, known/unknown/disputed status.
* `Consent` — record/version, signer, capacity and mandateRef, applicable authorities/rights, use grants and exclusions, dates, audience, duration, comprehension, jurisdiction, and disclosed retention/withdrawal terms (§3.3).
* `Person` — separate subject/contributor/collector/signer capacities; minimal PII; restricted encrypted contacts.
* `Node` — metadata graph entity: person, place, theme, language, institution. A link implies no stewardship appointment or permission.
* `PreservationOperation` — operational node with accountable operators, community agreements, custody/access responsibilities, readiness evidence, and incident/withdrawal contacts. Node Zero and Node One are defined in `docs/501c3.txt` §6 Stages 0 and 8. Partners may retain independent custody.
* `Pathway` — ordered authorized references + attributed annotations; restriction propagates to embeds, search, and playlists.
* `Assignment` — task with rubric, submissions, mentor sign-off and authority checks.
* `Translation` / `Derivative` — source/version, purpose, permissionRef, reviewer, attribution, distribution destinations, and withdrawal dependency.
* `CustodyCopy` — location/counterparty, permission and deposit agreement, retention/backup period, notification, restriction/deletion receipt.
* `LifecycleRequest` — requester and capacity evidence, action, affected uses/copies, protective hold, deadlines, decision, appeal, closure evidence.
* `Audit` — tamper-evident minimal action trail; sensitive evidence in a separate retention-controlled store. Public changelogs are redacted and may be omitted when disclosure itself creates harm.

This schema is an acceptance target, not the current TypeScript shape. `src/data/memories.ts` already contains display fields `sourceAuthority`, optional `legalRights`, and `consentWithdrawal`; they are not proof of validated authority or a working rights registry. `docs/memory.txt` identifies current fields and proposed additions.

**Storage abstraction**
Adapters for object storage (S3/GCS/Azure), vector DB (FAISS/pgvector/Weaviate), search (OpenSearch/Algolia), and RDB (Postgres/PlanetScale). Media never stored in the DB.

### 6.1) Explicit v1 Scope Boundaries (Execution Guardrails)

**In scope for v1 (ship target):**

* Public site (`/`, `/memory-project`, `/memories`, `/memories/:id`, `/about`, `/community-rights`, and adopted legal/consent procedures). Demonstration notices appear on every demo index card and detail page before any illustrative verification or consent text.
* Contributor sign-in (passwordless) and baseline roles (Visitor, Contributor, Curator, Staff).
* Memory ingestion MVP: quarantine, metadata and separate capacities, evidenced use-specific consent, authorized processing, human publication review, restricted/preservation-only handling, redaction, and dual control for sensitive material.
* Transcript generation + human correction workflow.
* Search v1: keyword + core facets (language, region, era, medium).
* Admin review queue with independent access/custody states, minimal tamper-evident events, and a tracked withdrawal/restriction/correction/deletion request queue.
* Complete preservation exports, integrity checks, encrypted separated backups, verified restoration, copy/derivative inventory, and restore-time replay of restrictions/deletions.
* Localized consent/withdrawal instructions, disclosed retention and backup windows, designated stewards and escalation/appeal contacts.
* Foundational accessibility/performance budgets and observability baseline.

**Out of scope for v1 (defer):**

* Advanced map/timeline exploration with offline tiles.
* Partner OAuth app ecosystem, public SDKs, and webhooks at scale.
* Full assignments/programs suite and translation memory optimization.
* Kiosk/interactive exhibits. Participation economies are not an institutional delivery commitment (§15).
* Multi-region active-active architecture beyond baseline reliability target.

**v1 completion definition:**

* Before real collection, demonstrate on non-sensitive test fixtures: applicable authority/capacity evidence; scoped permission checks; restricted records absent from public pages, search, API, and media; sensitivity review and redaction; withdrawal across dependent views/copies; deletion and backup expiry; full preservation export and successful restoration without reviving revoked access. Assign operators, approve regional consent/retention procedures, and record gate evidence and sign-off.
* After the gates pass, a contributor may entrust material for preservation only. A curator may publish only separately authorized, reviewed content. A visitor may discover and cite that authorized record with safe provenance and uncertainty shown. A valid request must remove or restrict affected access and generate a safe receipt under §12.
* Demo records are visibly labeled on index and detail pages; “Verified,” granted-consent claims, and pilot/impact counts require actual evidence. A prototype alone does not satisfy v1 collection readiness.

**Interaction patterns**

* Multi‑step wizards with progress, autosave, and clear exit/resume.

* Map & timeline toggles: keyboard navigable, captioned.

**Accessibility**

* WCAG 2.2 AA baseline. `aria-live` for background processing. Color contrast ≥ 4.5:1.
* Captions/transcripts mandatory for public audio/video.
* Critical actions explain reversibility; publication can be restricted promptly, while completed deletion may be irreversible. Record minimal safe reasons, with sensitive details retained separately only when authorized.

### 7.1) Product Copy Guardrails (UI + System Messaging)

* Default tone: clear, calm, respectful, precise; never sensational.
* Prefer stewardship language over growth language ("preserve", "review", "publish with consent").
* Avoid anthropomorphizing AI/system behavior; describe concrete actions and limits.
* Critical actions must explain consequence, reversibility, and next step in one concise block.
* Error copy must admit failure plainly, provide immediate recovery actions, and preserve user dignity.
* Consent and privacy copy must be explicit, plain-language, and localized before release.
* Avoid urgency gimmicks and startup cliches in CTA labels; prefer archival/institutional framing.
* For sensitive content notices: informative, non-graphic, and user-controlled (opt-in reveal where possible).

## 8) Example Use Cases & Flows

1. **Student Field Collector**

* Receives Assignment → verifies applicable authority and signer capacity → captures localized scoped consent → submits to quarantine → mentor and sensitivity review → preserve only, restrict, or separately authorize publication. A photo/signature alone does not establish capacity.

2. **Translator**

* Picks task → assisted by translation memory → submits → reviewer approves → attribution shown on Memory.

3. **Curator/Archivist**

* Filters new items by region/era → redacts sensitive details → links related Nodes → assembles a Pathway.

4. **Visitor/Researcher**

* Lands on `/memory-project` → explores timeline → opens Memory → reads transcript → cites with permalink + checksum.

5. **Partner Institution**

* Signs custody/access and withdrawal terms → passes readiness review → syncs only authorized metadata/media → receives lifecycle notifications and confirms downstream restriction/deletion. Independent custody does not grant TIRO unlimited distribution rights.

## 10) Security, Privacy, Governance

* **Threat model**: PII leakage, unauthorized access, scraping, link‑sharing of restricted content, supply‑chain dependencies.
* **Controls**: least‑privilege RBAC/ABAC; signed media URLs; regional data residency; encryption at rest/in transit; rate‑limits; WAF.
* **Governance**: proposed authority map in `docs/501c3.txt` §6 Stage 5. The fiduciary board governs the charity and delegates operations; ethics reviewers set review conditions and may pause harmful uses; regional stewards execute authorized protocols; source authorities and legal rights holders retain their applicable authority. Staff may impose protective restrictions, but no TIRO body may expand consent or override applicable source/legal rights. Delegations and appeals must be documented before collection.
* **Privacy procedures**: assess applicable law and regional protocols rather than claiming blanket GDPR/CCPA compliance. Consent receipts, subject requests, minimization, retention schedules, and deletion controls require implementation evidence. Restricted consent evidence and sensitive provenance follow disclosed retention rules; append-only operational logs contain no protected content.
* **Conflicts and appeals**: conflicted reviewers recuse; independent review and board escalation address TIRO handling decisions. Authority disputes remain restricted while unresolved; a TIRO appeal is not a mechanism to extinguish community or legal rights.

## 12) Content Lifecycle

Preservation and public access are independent. Use `publicationStatus`: not-published|review|published|restricted|withdrawn and `custodyStatus`: quarantined|preserved|deletion-pending|deleted. A withdrawn record may remain preserved only under an applicable disclosed basis. Mark in-scope content deleted only after active removal and backup expiry are complete; register any separately authorized or legally required restricted retention explicitly, without implying those copies were erased.

| Action | Meaning | Request and decision authority |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Withdraw publication / use | Stop affected public access and future distribution/use in TIRO-controlled systems; disable dependent derivatives, embeds, search, API, caches, and media links | Authorized requester named in receipt, affected rights holder, or authorized representative; steward verifies capacity and scope and executes cessation |
| Restrict | Narrow audience, uses, metadata, or location visibility; may be an immediate protective hold | Applicable source/rights authority requests; staff may restrict provisionally for harm or disputed authority; renewed access needs authorization and review |
| Correct | Add or revise an attributed factual/transcription/translation correction; preserve safe provenance and contested accounts | Source, subject, contributor, or researcher may flag an error; authorized curator reviews evidence and source permissions before changing material |
| Retain for preservation only | Keep a restricted preservation copy with no public access or unauthorized reuse | Requires recorded preservation grant or applicable legal/integrity duty disclosed at consent; responsible archivist records purpose, access, duration, and review |
| Delete | Remove affected content and derived copies under TIRO control; schedule authorized backup expiry | Applicable rights/subject authority or receipt-designated requester; steward verifies capacity, archives lead executes, and any applicable legal retention exception is documented and explained |

Anyone may report harm or disputed authority; accepting a report does not itself authorize disclosure, ownership reassignment, or deletion. Protective restriction does not wait for final identity resolution when continued exposure may cause harm. Unclear capacity is resolved using minimal evidence; do not require impossible documentation or publish identifying proof.

**Proposed response windows (calendar time, acceptance targets pending adoption):** acknowledge within 2 days of receipt; impose a protective hold on credible urgent exposure within 24 hours; cease the affected publication and future distribution under TIRO control within 7 days of verified withdrawal; resolve or issue a reasoned status/retention decision within 30 days of receipt. Notify authorized partners within 2 days of verified scope and seek restriction/deletion receipts within 30 days. Escalate overdue requests; send a progress update at least every 7 days while a request remains open. Capacity review must not be used to prolong harmful access.

Ordinary deletion from active stores and controlled derivatives is due within 30 days of verification. At consent disclose the maximum backup expiry; the proposed baseline is no more than 90 days after active deletion, unless a specific applicable legal retention obligation is identified. Such obligations are recorded with authority, access limits, duration, review/expiry, and notice; no generic “integrity” exception creates indefinite secret retention. If a jurisdiction or agreement requires faster handling, use that stricter limit. Before consent, tell the source which published or independently held copies TIRO cannot retrieve.

**Backups, caches, derivatives, and partners:** invalidate controlled public caches and revoke media links when access ceases; block affected derivatives and future processing; track destination copies and send scoped restriction/deletion notices. Backups remain inaccessible for ordinary use, expire on the disclosed schedule, and replay current restrictions/deletion instructions before any restoration is served. Partner agreements require compatible handling and acknowledgments. Record failed or unverifiable recovery honestly and escalate; independent public copies cannot be guaranteed erased.

**Audit and appeals:** retain minimal nonsensitive action receipts only under an approved schedule. Sensitive evidence, testimony, precise locations, identifiers, and consent documents are excluded from permanent logs and public changelogs. Safe withdrawal tombstones may be retained only if appropriate; metadata that exposes the source must itself be restricted or deleted. A public changelog is optional when disclosure would create harm. Refer handling appeals to non-conflicted ethics reviewers and then the board under `docs/501c3.txt` §6 Stage 5; no appeal or board override expands source consent. Notify requesters of decisions, retained material, limits, and available appeal routes.
