# TIRO art-direction standard (proposal 0.2)

Status: **Proposal for founder review.** Not adopted brand policy.
Revision date: 2026-10-16.
Authority: Defers to `/community-rights` and `docs/ethos.txt` (§7 UX and brand; Appendix B voice). Where they speak, they govern.
Companion: `docs/design/living-trace.md`.

Every image TIRO publishes makes a claim about where it came from. This standard keeps those claims true.

## The rule

**An image may be beautiful, but it may not lie about its origin.** Every visual is one of three things, and must look like what it is:

1. **Evidence:** authentic material from a source, published with authorization. It carries its provenance.
2. **Demonstration:** synthetic content showing how the system works. It is labeled before anything that looks like a claim.
3. **Abstraction:** original geometry expressing an idea. It depicts no person, place, culture, or artifact.

When authentic, authorized material is unavailable, use honest abstraction or a labeled demonstration. Never use a convincing substitute.

## By medium

| Medium | Use | Never |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Abstract geometry** | The six primitives (origin, trace, constellation, boundary, time, voice), each for its one meaning. Square-cornered, fine-stroked, mostly mineral. Gold only for an origin's own line. | Geometry as texture or wallpaper; a line that implies a relationship the data lacks; "network" imagery without typed links. |
| **Original illustration** | Diagrams that explain a system: provenance, consent scope, lifecycle. Labeled as diagrams. | Illustrated people, faces, or scenes standing in for contributors; stylized "cultural" figures. |
| **Photography and portraiture** | Only consent-cleared images of real people, credited as they choose, with use scope recorded. Context beside the image, not in a hidden caption. | Stock photography of people presented as TIRO's contributors or communities; aestheticized poverty or hardship; portraits used as mood; faces generated or composited. |
| **Archival objects and documents** | Real items with authorization, shown with their record (source, authority, rights, access). Scans shown as scans, unretouched. | Invented artifacts; "aged" textures that suggest archival origin; a real object shown without its record. |
| **Audio and waveforms** | A waveform only for audio that exists and is authorized, responding to real playback. Opt-in, never autoplay, always with a transcript or captions. With no audio, show the silent waveform ("No audio attached"). | Decorative or invented waveforms; ambient voices; an empty player that implies a recording exists. |
| **Maps, timelines, spatial relationships** | Places and dates at the precision the source authorizes; uncertainty drawn as uncertainty (dotted, ranges). A timeline is the record's own history. | Precise locations of sensitive or restricted material; chronology implying that a culture is linear or "developing"; maps as decoration. |
| **Institutional and research interfaces** | The Paper register: high legibility, structured fields, evidence states in words and line patterns, generous measure. Archival precision before atmosphere. | Cinematic effects inside records; colour as the only carrier of meaning; dashboards that turn people into metrics. |

## The mark

The approved mark is never altered, recolored outside the brand tokens, animated, or redrawn. The Origin's annotations (Origin / Transmission / Resonance, cited to Manifesto v0.2 §IV) are **approved as explanatory artwork** for that composition (`decisions.md`, decision 5). They sit beside the mark and do not change it. They are **not** part of ordinary logo usage and must never become a required lockup.

## Culture and context

- **No generic motifs.** No "African" patterns, masks, adinkra or nsibidi symbols, textiles, sculpture, or script used as ornament, and no other culture's symbols used that way either. A culturally specific symbol appears only inside a record, with its source authority, its meaning as the source states it, and permission to show it.
- **No invented cultural practices.** Demonstration content never invents a ritual, song, custom, or tradition and attributes it to a real community. Synthetic demonstrations use abstract labels ("Source S-01", "Place").
- **Name real places only when you need to.** In demonstrations, prefer abstract placeholders. A real place named in a demo must not suggest TIRO has worked there.
- **Plurality, not composites.** Never merge several sources into one "representative" person, voice, or image.

## Generative and synthetic media

- Generated imagery, audio, or text is never presented as archival evidence, testimony, or a contributor's likeness.
- Synthetic reproduction of a real voice or likeness requires the distinct authorization in `docs/ethos.txt` §3.3. Brand use is not an exception.
- Any synthetic media that is used is labeled as synthetic where it appears, not only in a credit line.

## The two registers

The public site (Encounter → Participate) may be atmospheric and narrative. The Memory Project's records are precise, accessible, and evidence-centered. They are related registers of one identity, not one interface. A visitor should recognize both as TIRO, and archival legibility is never traded for cinematic expression inside a record.

## Review checklist (before publishing any visual)

1. Is it evidence, demonstration, or abstraction, and does it look like that?
2. If evidence: is its use authorized for this audience and channel? Is provenance shown?
3. If demonstration: is it labeled before any claim-like detail? Is it free of real identities and invented culture?
4. Does every line or connection have a type and a basis?
5. Is it complete without motion, sound, or colour?
6. Could it embarrass, endanger, or misrepresent a source or community if it travelled without context?
