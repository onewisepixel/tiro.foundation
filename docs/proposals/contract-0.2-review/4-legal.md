## Issues for your answer

### RI-4 (High) — Staff access basis
**The contract today.** Cases show staff seeing all material, while the general rule requires every interest's authorization.

**Proposed** (see packet 1): a minimal, audited administrative-review basis tied to named custody duties.

**We ask:**
1. What lawful basis supports staff access to personal data and restricted material for custody duties, in the relevant jurisdictions? Is it consent, legitimate interest, or a legal obligation?
2. What purpose limitation, minimization, logging, and data-subject notice does that basis require?
3. Must access to some categories (for example, health information, or religious or cultural practice information) be further restricted?

### RI-3 (Critical) — Safety restrictions on locations
**Proposed** (see packet 1): a location-level safety restriction affecting independently sourced records requires documented justification of risk and independent review, distinct from an association restriction under a community's own authority.

**We ask:**
1. What legal exposure arises from suppressing, or from disclosing, a sensitive location held in an independent record?
2. Are there recognized collective or customary rights, or cultural-heritage protections, in the relevant jurisdictions that bear on this? Do they conflict with P14?

### RI-1 (Critical) — contribution requested
Does disclosing that a claim **exists** (without content) from a restricted record itself constitute disclosure of personal data, or of protected information, in some cases? For example, where the mere existence of an account about a person is sensitive.

## Founder positions for you to confirm or challenge

### Position 28 — Interpretive enrichment within `derivatives`
Interpretive enrichment is a separately authorized scope within `derivatives`, not a ninth use category, and is not authorized by a generic derivatives grant. `ethos.txt` is not amended pending your review.

**We ask:** Annotation, scholarly commentary, translation, and adaptation may differ legally (copyright, moral rights, performers' rights, fair use or fair dealing). Is one scope adequate for TIRO's operational standard, given that it is intentionally stricter than the law requires? Or do some of these need distinct scopes?

### Position 29 — Explicit references; unreviewed content suppressed
**We ask:** Does suppressing unreviewed unstructured content until review satisfy, or exceed, privacy obligations around identifying information in transcripts?
