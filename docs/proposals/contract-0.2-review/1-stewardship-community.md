## Issues for your answer

### RI-3 (Critical) — Community restriction vs independently sourced records
**The contract today (case F).** Community authority sets Place V to "withheld". The place associations of R8 (entrusted under that authority) **and** R9 (a different source's record) are both suppressed.

**The concern.** P14 says authority over entrusted material is not a power to suppress independently sourced information. Case F may let one party silence another party's account.

**Proposed distinction for your judgment:**

| Restriction type | Applies to | Basis | Review |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Association restriction** | Associations TIRO holds **under that authority's own entrusted material** | The authority's interest in its own material (§7.1) | Standard |
| **Safety restriction on a location** | The location itself, across all records | A documented risk (harm to people, a site, or a practice), never ownership or cultural authority alone | Separate, independently reviewed, time-limited, and recorded with reasons |

**We ask:**
1. Is this distinction right? Is anything missing, for example sacred or ceremonial sites, or locations of vulnerable people?
2. Who may request a safety restriction, and who decides? Should the affected independent source be heard?
3. What should a visitor see when an independent record's place association is withheld for safety? The contract currently shows nothing.

### RI-4 (High) — Staff access
**The contract today.** Cases B–E show staff seeing "everything". The general rule requires every interest's authorization.

**Proposed for your judgment.** A separate **administrative-review basis**:
- limited to a named custody duty (intake review, withdrawal processing, dispute facilitation, preservation checks);
- the minimum fields needed;
- time-limited;
- logged with a safe receipt;
- never extended to research, publication, or curiosity.

**We ask:**
1. Which custody duties genuinely need access to restricted content, and which need only metadata?
2. Should sources or communities be told when staff access their material under this basis? Always, on request, or never?
3. Should some material be inaccessible even to staff (for example, knowledge restricted by gender, initiation, or lineage), with TIRO holding only an opaque preservation copy?

### RI-1 (Critical) — contribution requested
When a record is restricted, may a community ever authorize acknowledging, inside another record's relationship, that one of its claims **exists**? Decision 2 permits this only with explicit, separately scoped authorization. Is that ever appropriate, or should restricted material never be acknowledged at all?

## Founder positions for you to confirm or challenge

### Position 27 — Evidence of community authority (Nigerian pilot)
Documented consultation recording:
- how representatives are selected;
- the boundaries of their mandates;
- how dissent is handled;
- who may withdraw or challenge an authorization.

No agreement is permanently irrevocable because its first representatives were recognized. No single governance form is presumed.

**We ask:** What should that documentation contain for the communities expected to participate? What makes it adequate, and who in the community should confirm it? How should a change of representatives affect authorizations already given?

### Position 28 — Interpretive enrichment
Interpretation is a separately authorized scope within `derivatives`; a generic derivatives grant does not cover it.

**We ask:** Does this protect communities enough? Are there kinds of interpretation (for example, explaining a ritual's meaning) that should need community authorization even when the source individual agrees?
