TIRO FOUNDATION — NODE ZERO

There is enlightenment in exploration, but empathy is essential.

TIRO is a permanent, neutral custodian of endangered cultural and linguistic memory — built to outlast the people, institutions, and technologies that built it.

Mission

Civilization is not merely what humanity builds.
It is what humanity manages not to forget.

Every time a language falls silent, an elder dies unheard, or a community loses control of its own history, humanity's inheritance becomes smaller. TIRO exists to resist that loss — not by owning what it preserves, but by holding it in trust.

Our responsibility is not to a technology. It is to what technology can help humanity preserve, understand, and transmit before it is gone.

The Custodian Doctrine

To record something does not make it ours.

TIRO approaches memory as a custodian, not an owner. Custody should never confer the authority to rewrite, exploit, commercialize, or erase what has been entrusted to it.

Preservation without appropriation

We collect not for curation, but for connection — and the people from whom knowledge came retain their rights, intentions, and dignity in it.

Access without dispossession

Making knowledge accessible must never require the communities who hold it to surrender control of it.

Technology without techno-solutionism

Capability does not guarantee wisdom. We ask not only whether something can be built, but who benefits, whose knowledge it contains, and what must remain human even when automation becomes possible.

Permanence over virality

A system designed for a quarter behaves differently from one designed for a century. TIRO is built for people we will never meet.

Flagship Program

The Memory Project

Recording, digitizing, transcribing, translating, and preserving oral and cultural material — voices, rituals, songs, techniques, and stories — with the people to whom it belongs, not apart from them.

The work moves from a single, bounded community outward: collection, consent, provenance, preservation, contextualization, public access, and community benefit, proven once before it is ever scaled.

Provenance & Rights

Communities remain the principals. TIRO is the custodian.

Nothing enters the archive without documented provenance: who contributed it, under what terms, and with what continuing rights. What belongs to a people does not become ours merely because we preserved it.

Where a community sets conditions on access, translation, or reuse, those conditions travel with the material — permanently, not as a courtesy that can later be revised away.

TIRO Nodes

A node is not a center. It is a point of connection.

A node listens, remembers, and bears witness — receiving what has been entrusted to it and transmitting only what it has the right to transmit. A person, a village, a library, or a university can each be a node. TIRO is one node among them, not the center they orbit.

The ambition is not a single repository into which the world's memory disappears. It is a network through which memory can survive.

Governance

The Steward protects the constitution. The Board governs the charity.

TIRO's founder holds a narrow, constitutional role — Steward-Protector — reserved for decisions that would alter TIRO's founding mission: dissolution, sale of archival assets, or abandonment of the custodian principles. It is a brake, not a veto over ordinary governance.

Objection raised→ 90-day pause→ independent review→ board reconsideration→ supermajority override
