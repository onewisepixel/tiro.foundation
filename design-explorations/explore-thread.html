TIRO Foundation — founding document

Some things are worth remembering on purpose.

TIRO is a permanent, neutral custodian of endangered cultural and linguistic memory — built to outlast the people, institutions, and technologies that built it.

Mission

Civilization is not only what we build. It's what we manage not to forget.

Every time a language falls silent or a community loses control of its own history, humanity's inheritance gets smaller. TIRO exists to resist that loss — not by owning what it preserves, but by holding it in trust.

The Custodian Doctrine

To record something does not make it ours.

Preservation without appropriation

The people from whom knowledge came retain their rights, intentions, and dignity in it.

Access without dispossession

Making knowledge findable must never require its keepers to surrender control of it.

Technology without techno-solutionism

We ask not only whether something can be built, but who benefits, and what must stay human even once automation is possible.

Provenance & Rights

Communities remain the principals. TIRO is the custodian.

Nothing enters the archive without documented provenance — who contributed it, under what terms, with what continuing rights. Where a community sets conditions on access or reuse, those conditions travel with the record permanently, not as a courtesy that can be revised away later.

Governance

The Steward protects the constitution. The Board governs the charity.

TIRO's founder holds one narrow, constitutional role — Steward-Protector — reserved only for decisions that would alter TIRO's founding mission. It's a brake, not a veto over ordinary governance.
